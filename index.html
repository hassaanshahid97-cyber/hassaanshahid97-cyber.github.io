Research

How wrong is a fast surrogate allowed to be?

A physics-informed surrogate is easy to demonstrate and hard to qualify. This study answers the two questions that decide whether one is usable: how much of the reported accuracy survives a change of random seed, and how large an error allowance a decision rule needs to stay safe.

Seed variability and calibrated error bounds for physics-informed conjugate heat transfer surrogates

Preprint Code

Sole-authored, written and executed independently over one summer. Submitted to a peer-reviewed journal in August 2026, not yet reviewed. Code, data, figures and an audit script released under MIT; the manuscript under CC BY 4.0. The whole study reproduces from a clean checkout in under two core-hours.

Finding 1

The physics residual substitutes for reference data. Given four CHT solutions it improves stress error 3.5 times, and with none at all it still beats a supervised model given four.

Finding 2

It collapses run-to-run variance, seed standard deviation of field RMSE from 12.42 K to 0.27 K. A single-seed study cannot see this.

Finding 3

An accurate surrogate is not a safe one. As a hard constraint it broke the structural limit on 14.4 per cent of manoeuvres; a split-conformal allowance restored the nominal 5 per cent.

Where it loses

Reported because it is what the experiment shows: given 24 reference solutions, a plain supervised model was twice as accurate in-envelope.
